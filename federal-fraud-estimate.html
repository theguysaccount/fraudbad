The government's own auditors estimate fraud costs taxpayers $233 billion to $521 billion a year.

What happened

In April 2024 the Government Accountability Office published its first estimate of fraud across the whole federal government. It put the loss at $233 billion to $521 billion every year.

That is 3 to 7 percent of everything the government commits to spend. GAO built it from five years of data, 2018 through 2022.

Why nobody caught it

  • Agencies don't measure fraud. They measure "improper payments," which mixes fraud with honest mistakes and missing paperwork.
  • Fraud only gets counted once somebody is caught. GAO's estimate is an attempt to size what never gets caught.
  • The estimate leaves out fraud against tax revenue, and fraud in state-run programs unless federal investigators reported it.

How sure are we?

Estimated

An auditor, inspector general or the agency itself estimated the fraud. It's a measured range, not a list of names.

This is a statistical range, not a list of cases. GAO says not to apply the percentages to any single agency or program, and the real number could fall outside the range.
