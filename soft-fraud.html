Bigger than the crimes

Soft fraud

Hard fraud is someone stealing. Soft fraud is everyone shrugging: money paid without proof, paid too much, paid twice, lost track of, or never collected. No one has to break a law, so no one gets charged — and it runs into the trillions.

The method

How we search for soft fraud

Seven signals. Each one names where to look and what to measure, so anyone can repeat the search and check our work.

01

Paid without proof

Look for payments that went out without the records to show they were right.

Where we look
Agency improper-payment estimates and their "insufficient documentation" share; CMS error-rate reports.
What we measure
Share of dollars paid without documentation.
$95.5 billion: Health care money paid with no proof it was right →$2.98 trillion since 2004: Paid out without proof, year after year →$123 billion paid wrong: The data to stop it existed. Nobody checked. →$10.1 billion paid wrong or unproven: Venue grants: 69% paid wrong or without proof →$4.35 billion HUD can't verify: Rent subsidies HUD can't verify →
02

Paid more than everyone else

Compare what one buyer paid with what others paid for the same thing.

Where we look
Unit prices on federal orders (the RECEIPTS Act would publish these), inspector-general price audits, MedPAC's Medicare Advantage vs. traditional Medicare comparison.
What we measure
Price paid ÷ median price for the same item.
$76 billion in 2026: What Medicare overpays private insurance plans →$3.6 billion in one year: Medicare paid far more for cheap generics than an online pharmacy charges →
03

Over budget, behind schedule

Compare today's cost and date with what was promised.

Where we look
GAO's annual weapons assessment, the federal IT Dashboard, state and city capital-project audits.
What we measure
Current cost estimate ÷ original baseline; years of delay.
$122 billion in new cost growth: Weapons programs that went over budget this year →$100 billion+ a year: Federal tech money spent keeping old systems alive →$141 billion, 81% over estimate: The nuclear missile that's 81% over budget →$126–231 billion, from $33B promised: California's bullet train: $33 billion promised, up to $231 billion now →$10.1 billion, double the plan: Honolulu's rail line: twice the cost, a mile shorter →
04

Paying twice

Find programs that do the same job for the same people.

Where we look
GAO's annual duplication, overlap and fragmentation report and its open recommendations.
What we measure
Dollars in overlapping programs; GAO's estimated savings.
$132–251 billion left on the table: Savings the government's auditors found — and nobody took →
05

Owed and never collected

Count money the government is owed and never gets.

Where we look
IRS tax gap projections; Treasury's yearly report on delinquent non-tax debt; uncollected overpayment balances in agency financial reports.
What we measure
Money owed minus money collected.
$696 billion a year: Taxes owed that never get paid →$217 billion owed and overdue: Money owed to the government that it isn't collecting →$23 billion not yet recovered: Social Security overpayments that haven't come back →
06

Can't show where it went

Find spending nobody can trace or explain.

Where we look
Failed financial audits, USAspending.gov fields left blank, local audits that can't match spending to results.
What we measure
Dollars with no traceable recipient, purpose or result.
$160.4 billion with no description: Giant contracts the public can't read →$4.65 trillion in assets: Eight audits. Eight failures. →$2.3 billion: Homelessness money LA couldn't fully trace →
07

Spend it or lose it

Watch for spending rushed out before the money expires.

Where we look
Contract obligations by fiscal month on USAspending.gov. Research found agencies spend 4.9 times a normal week's amount in the last week of the fiscal year, and last-week IT projects were 2 to 6 times more likely to be rated lower quality.
What we measure
September's share of the year's contract obligations vs. 1/12 (8.3%).
$162 billion in one month: The September rush: spend it before it expires →

America.co · The RECEIPTS Act

Want to stop it?Sign the Receipts Act.

Public money should come with a receipt anyone can check. The RECEIPTS Act starts with one rule for federal purchases over $25,000 from GSA's buying catalog: publish the price paid next to what other agencies paid for the same thing.
