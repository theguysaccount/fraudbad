$76 billion in 2026: What Medicare overpays private insurance plans

Medicare's own advisers say it will pay about $76 billion more this year for people in private Medicare Advantage plans than it would in traditional Medicare.

What happened

Private Medicare Advantage plans get paid more for sicker patients, based on the diagnoses the plans report. MedPAC, Congress's Medicare advisers, estimates Medicare will spend about 14 percent more on these enrollees in 2026, roughly $76 billion.

In January 2026, Kaiser Permanente affiliates agreed to pay $556 million to settle allegations that they pressured doctors to add diagnoses patients didn't have. The complaint alleged about half a million added diagnoses and about $1 billion in improper payments.

Projected forward with the Congressional Budget Office's baseline, those overpayments come to about $1.3 trillion from 2027 through 2036: roughly $520 billion from coding and $760 billion from favorable selection, by the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget's count.

Why nobody caught it

  • The diagnoses that set the payment come from the plans themselves.
  • Most of Medicare Advantage's $23.67 billion in improper payments in fiscal 2025 came from records that didn't back up the diagnoses the plans reported.

How sure are we?

Settled

Money was paid to settle the government's claims, without an admission of wrongdoing.

MedPAC's $76 billion is an overpayment estimate, and some of it is legal under current rules. Kaiser did not admit wrongdoing.
