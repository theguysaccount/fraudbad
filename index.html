ConfirmedProven in court, admitted in a guilty plea, or confirmed by the agency that paid it.
ChargedProsecutors filed charges.
SettledMoney was paid to settle the government's claims, without an admission of wrongdoing.
EstimatedAn auditor, inspector general or the agency itself estimated the fraud.
Paid without proofOfficially counted as paid in error or without the records to prove it was right.
Payments frozenOfficials stopped payment over suspected fraud.
Can't be checkedThe books or the spending can't be verified, so fraud can't be ruled in or out.
Over budgetOfficially measured cost growth or delay beyond what was promised.
Fix ignoredA watchdog found the waste and the fix.
MeasuredFraudBad counted it ourselves from official government data.
Never collectedMoney the government is owed and hasn't collected, by its own count.

Soft fraud

Money lost without anyone breaking a law: paid without proof, paid too much, paid twice, lost track of, or never collected. Each of these is an official or peer-reviewed number.

America.co · The RECEIPTS Act

Want to stop it?Sign the Receipts Act.

Public money should come with a receipt anyone can check. The RECEIPTS Act starts with one rule for federal purchases over $25,000 from GSA's buying catalog: publish the price paid next to what other agencies paid for the same thing.
