What California's auditors found

$1.5BUnemployment insurance · from the auditor's own report

The Employment Development Department's estimated improper unemployment payments came to about $1.5 billion over 2023 and 2024, and the auditor put fraud at more than $500 million in 2024 alone.

California State Auditor: State High-Risk Audit Program (2025-601). Filed Dec 11, 2025. How we picked this.

Unemployment benefits paid wrong

8.83%of unemployment benefits overpaid or underpaid · #34 of 51, #1 worst

In the 12 months ending March 31, 2026, California paid 8.83% of its unemployment benefits in the wrong amount, down from 12.06% a year earlier. The national rate was 12.11%. The federal goal is under 10%; California is under it.

The same sample found 5.53% of benefits went out on payments the Labor Department counts as fraud (national: 4.00%).

The Labor Department checks a random sample of each state's payments every year (its Benefit Accuracy Measurement program). Like the SNAP rate, "paid wrong" counts paying too much and too little.

Public money should come with a receipt anyone can check. The RECEIPTS Act starts with one rule for federal purchases over $25,000 from GSA's buying catalog: publish the price paid next to what other agencies paid for the same thing.
