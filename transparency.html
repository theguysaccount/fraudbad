What one receipt shows

The $149,072 soap dispensers

When the Pentagon's inspector general compared what the Air Force paid for 46 spare parts against what they should cost, it found a soap dispenser marked up 7,943%. For 25 of the 46 parts, the Air Force couldn't even say whether the price was fair.

Nobody needed an investigation to see it. They needed the price paid next to what everyone else pays. The RECEIPTS Act would make that kind of comparison public, starting with federal orders over $25,000 placed through GSA's schedules.

America.co · The RECEIPTS Act

Want to stop it?Sign the Receipts Act.

Public money should come with a receipt anyone can check. The RECEIPTS Act starts with one rule for federal purchases over $25,000 from GSA's buying catalog: publish the price paid next to what other agencies paid for the same thing.
