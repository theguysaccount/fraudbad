What every state's auditors found

The biggest problem each state's own audit put a dollar figure on, read from the report itself.

Every state that spends $1 million or more in federal money a year must get a single audit: an outside check of how it spent that money, filed with the federal government. Most states' own auditors do it; some hire accounting firms. When auditors find spending that broke the rules or that the state couldn't back up with records, they list it as questioned costs.

For each state we read its latest single audit (49 of 51 rows) and show the largest questioned cost in it, or the report's own total when it gives one. Where the state's auditor published a bigger finding in its own report, we show that instead and say so. "Projected" means the auditors found a small amount in a sample and estimated the rest; we show both numbers.

Questioned costs are not fraud findings. Some are bookkeeping errors. They show where the receipts didn't hold up. 8 states' audits put no dollar figure on any finding.

Public money should come with a receipt anyone can check. The RECEIPTS Act starts with one rule for federal purchases over $25,000 from GSA's buying catalog: publish the price paid next to what other agencies paid for the same thing.
