$18 billion billed: Minnesota programs where prosecutors say half may be fraud

A federal prosecutor says "half or more" of the $18 billion billed to 14 high-risk Minnesota Medicaid programs since 2018 may be fraudulent. The state's own watchdog disputes it.

What happened

In December 2025 an assistant U.S. attorney said fraud in 14 high-risk, state-run Medicaid programs could be half or more of the $18 billion billed since 2018. Minnesota shut down its Housing Stabilization Services program in August 2025, citing credible allegations of fraud.

Charges have followed in housing, autism and disability programs. In May 2026 the Justice Department charged 15 more people over $90 million across seven programs.

Why nobody caught it

  • Some programs grew from millions to hundreds of millions of dollars in a few years. Integrated Community Supports paid out $4.6 million in 2021 and $170 million in 2024.
  • Providers billed for services that, prosecutors say, were never delivered.

How sure are we?

Charged

Prosecutors filed charges. These are accusations; everyone charged is presumed innocent until proven guilty.

The "half or more" figure is a prosecutor's estimate. The state Department of Human Services inspector general called it speculation. Charges are accusations until proven in court.
