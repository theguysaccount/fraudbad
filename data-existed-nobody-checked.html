$123 billion paid wrong: The data to stop it existed. Nobody checked.

Two-thirds of the government's $185.8B in improper payments in fiscal 2025 happened because the agency didn't check information it already had access to — by the government's own count.

What happened

Every federal program that reports improper payments has to say why they happened, using categories set by the Office of Management and Budget.

The biggest category is "failure to access data." OMB defines it as errors made even though the information needed to check the payment "exists and is accessible" to the agency. In fiscal 2025 that category alone was $123.0B — 66% of the total.

Why nobody caught it

  • 15 of the 63 agencies tracked were out of compliance with the Payment Integrity Information Act in fiscal 2024, including Health and Human Services, Treasury, Social Security, Agriculture, Defense, Veterans Affairs, HUD, SBA and Labor.
  • In fiscal 2025 agencies flagged $77.5B in overpayments to claw back and recovered $47.8B.

How sure are we?

Paid without proof

Officially counted as paid in error or without the records to prove it was right. Some is fraud. Nobody has measured how much.

Root causes are reported by each agency to OMB. FraudBad summed them from the official FY2025 dataset; the total matches the $186 billion GAO reports. These describe errors, not intent.
